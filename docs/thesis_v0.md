## 3. Criteria (working definitions)

"Observable at intake" = whether a deck + form can show it, without a partner call.

### C1. Founder–market fit (earned insight)
- **Definition:** founders have done unscalable, close-to-the-customer work and can describe what customers actually want in the customer's terms.
- **Strong:** field visits, manual first operations, failures described unprompted [M3 Unbox, M5 Wint, M9 SuperK, M11 Jai Kisan].
- **Weak:** credentials or passion standing in for evidence [M8 AeroDrop, N3 "credentials are not a business"].
- **Observable at intake:** partly (stated background and customer-contact history; not the behaviour in a call).

### C2. Founder owns the key risk
- **Definition:** the founder treats the business's central risk (credit, regulation, liability, unit economics) as theirs to solve, not someone else's.
- **Strong:** Jai Kisan built credit-risk depth plan [M11]; ePlane gave milestone sequence for regulation [M1].
- **Weak:** "the NBFC partner handles it" [M6 CampusCredit]; "solved once the tech works" [M8]; "rules will become friendlier" [M10].
- **Observable at intake:** partly (form language on funding, first-loss, regulation, liability).
- **Note:** this is the single most repeated decider (M1, M6, M8, M10, M11, N2).

### C3. Commercial fluency (numbers the founder owns)
- **Definition:** founder knows cohort repeat, margins and channel CAC without notes. A missing number counts against the score; it is not skipped [N1].
- **Strong:** Giva founder gave cohort repeat and gross margin from memory [M7].
- **Weak:** channel-level averages, no repeat rate, "content is great" as the retention answer [M2 Petalry, M12 Prepnest].
- **Observable at intake:** yes (deck/form either contain the metric or they do not).

### C4. Evidence of customer and path to market (kept separate from founder quality)
- **Definition:** a real, named paying or piloting customer, and a credible route from pilot to rollout.
- **Strong:** live customer sites [M3]; pilots treated as learning.
- **Weak:** no signed customer, no buyer identified [M8].
- **Rule:** score separately from C1 ("agreed to separate founder quality from path to market" [N3]).
- **Observable at intake:** yes.

### C5. Regulatory path clarity
- **Definition:** regulation is acknowledged, bounded, and the founders have a sequence for dealing with it.
- **Strong:** ePlane: long path but a concrete milestone sequence [M1, N3].
- **Weak:** AeroDrop (no regulator-facing plan); Coinlane (depends on a favourable rule change) [M8, M10].
- **Observable at intake:** partly; needs external research on the actual regulatory position.

### C6. Capital intensity vs a pre-seed cheque
- **Definition:** the model must be able to work on capital the fund deploys, without a large raise to reach density, and without the fund being squeezed later.
- **Strong:** ePlane cheque sized so a slow regulatory path "does not force a bad financing" [M1].
- **Weak:** HopBox: works only at density, negative contribution margin [M4, N1].
- **Observable at intake:** yes (ask size, use of funds, unit economics).
- **Caution:** the fund's cheque size is unknown. See open questions.

### C7. Team completeness
- **Definition:** commercial and operational/technical cover; the weak spot is named.
- **Strong:** complementary pairs [M1, M3, M9].
- **Weak:** solo founder with no ops cover [M2]; weak commercial co-founder [M8]; classmates with no credit experience [M6].
- **Observable at intake:** yes (team page and form).

### C8. Defensibility / openness under challenge
- **Definition:** founder engages with the weak spot; a defensive reaction is itself a signal [N1].
- **Weak:** Prepnest: product is the teacher, founder bristled [M12].
- **Observable at intake:** mostly no (call behaviour). Intake proxies only: candour about risks, consistency between deck and form.

### C9. Sector and category fit
- **Definition:** fits fintech / deeptech / consumer tech / consumer brands, or is justified by founder behaviour despite the label.
- **Evidence:** Jai Kisan rural credit accepted: "founder behaviour mattered more than the sector label" [M11, N2]. Coinlane declined on category alone: "no founder quality could change that" [M10].
- **Observable at intake:** yes for the label; the category judgement needs research.

## 4. Scoring rules taken from the notes
1. A missing key number drags the score. It is never skipped [N1].
2. Score the evidence, not the pedigree [N3].
3. Separate founder quality from path to market [N3].
4. A founder getting defensive on a weak spot is recorded [N1].
5. Every correction needs a one-line reason so it can be reused on similar companies [N5].
6. State which thesis was used (written or revealed) and why [N5].
7. Nobody is auto-rejected or auto-advanced; Kushal sees every outcome [N5].

## 7. Tensions that stay open
1. **Written vs revealed thesis [N5].** Riya: recent yeses (small-town retail, founder-led fixed income) would score lower on the public wording than several passes. Aditya: thesis stable, pipeline changed. Kushal: both could be true. Unreconciled.
2. **Stage confound.** All six investments are labelled "Early round" and all six passes "Pre-seed", while the brief says the fund invests at pre-seed. From the files alone this cannot be settled. Public research (section 9) shows the fund invests at pre-seed AND seed, which fits the labels, but whether stage acted as a hidden criterion remains open.
3. **Hindsight in the memos.** The "why we decided" text was written after the call. A backtest must not feed the system that text.
4. **Founder behaviour is unobservable at intake.** The fund's primary variable comes from calls. At intake the system can only score proxies and must say so.
5. **Weights.** The files give no way to weight C1–C9. Do not invent them.

## 9. Public layer (from `fund_public_research.md`)

**Where public wording agrees with the memos**
- Founder-market fit is the stated core: what founders know and can do, the key risk, "earned secrets" (Inc42 interview, 2023). Matches C1 and C2.
- Complementary, long-collaborating teams are liked. Matches C7.
- "No safe bets"; risk is managed by price and volume. Supports C6 (entry valuation and capital need matter).
- Avoids later-stage "safe" deals. Supports a stage flag for Series A applicants.

**New criterion from public wording**
- **C10. Why now.** The fund starts with "what has changed in the world that makes this possible now" (a technology or behaviour shift). The 12 memos never mention it. Observable at intake: yes (deck's why-now slide). Weight unknown; flagged as present in public but absent from revealed decisions.

**Where public facts differ from the case brief or memos**
- Stage is pre-seed **and seed**, not pre-seed only. Cheques: official range USD 250-600K (conflicting figures across sources), Fund II first cheques up to INR 5 cr.
- The official portfolio is dominated by B2B software and commerce infrastructure, more than by the four sectors in the brief. This supports Riya's drift observation [N5].
- The portfolio includes Web3 names (QuestBook, Spiti.xyz), so the Coinlane gate in section 5 should be read as "business depends on favourable regulation" (retail crypto exchange), not "Web3 is excluded".
- The six invested memo companies and MeetRecord are all on the official portfolio page. The six passes were not found.

**Still not public:** ownership target, exclusions, scoring, the fund's own application questions (document not opened), and the full portfolio.
