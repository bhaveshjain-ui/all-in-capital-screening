# FACT SHEETS (set 4). Neutral facts from a past screening. Business type is given for each.

## Case: "Coinlane" — business type: regulation-dependent (retail crypto)
- A retail crypto trading app and wallet for Indian users, with fiat on-ramp through a payment partner. Growing user base from referral campaigns; the user growth was real.
- Founders: Siddharth Menon, previously a growth lead at a payments company, and a co-founder with a blockchain engineering background. In the meeting he spoke confidently about regulation; his answers assumed the rules would become friendlier, and no plan was stated for the scenario in which they did not.
- Risks listed by the partners: regulatory and tax uncertainty on crypto in India; banking and payment-partner dependency; trading revenue is cyclical; intense competition from large, well-funded exchanges.

## Case: "Jai Kisan" — business type: lending / credit (rural)
- A fintech platform providing financing to farmers and rural households for income-generating purchases such as agricultural and dairy equipment. Mumbai-based, founded 2017.
- Founders: Arjun Ahluwalia and Adriel Maniego. They built a presence across many channel-partner locations in multiple districts, with thousands of farmers registered and assessed before they scaled lending. A concern raised internally was that credit-risk depth would need to be built or hired quickly as lending scaled.
- Risks listed by the partners: rural credit risk and collections; regulatory exposure; dependence on channel partners; seasonality tied to monsoon and crop cycles.

## Case: "Prepnest" — business type: other (consumer app, exam preparation)
- A mobile-first test-preparation app for competitive exams, with video lessons and adaptive practice. Strong early download numbers.
- Founders: Neha Bhatt, a former teacher with a popular online following, and a technical co-founder. On retention, her answer was that students "keep coming back because the content is great"; she did not have 30- or 90-day numbers. She bristled at a question about what happens once a larger player copies the format.
- Risks listed by the partners: crowded category with entrenched, heavily funded players; retention and conversion to paid not shown; content-led, founder-dependent product that is hard to scale beyond one teacher.
