# FACT SHEETS (set 1). Neutral facts from a past screening. Business type is given for each.

## Case: "ePlane" — business type: deeptech
- Makes electric vertical take-off and landing (eVTOL) aircraft and unmanned aerial vehicles for short-range intracity travel. Incubated at IIT Madras; grew out of a combustion research lab; founded 2016.
- Founders: Pranjal Mehta (CEO, commercial operator) and Satyanarayanan Chakravarthy (CTO, deep technical background). Both have stayed with the problem for years. Neither has shipped a certified aircraft; neither has run a large manufacturing organisation. Asked "what would make you quit?", they answered with a specific technical milestone.
- Risks listed by the partners: regulation and certification timelines are long and outside the founders' control; capital intensity is high and first revenue is far away; market sizing in the deck was aspirational rather than evidenced.

## Case: "Petalry" — business type: consumer brand (D2C skincare)
- Direct-to-consumer skincare brand selling through its own site and marketplaces. About 18 months old; a few thousand monthly orders; strong Instagram presence.
- Anika Rao, solo founder, formerly a brand manager at a large FMCG company. She said she had never owned a P&L and had no co-founder covering operations or supply chain. Asked unit-economics questions, she answered with channel-level averages, not cohort data. Repeat rate is not tracked.
- Risks listed by the partners: customer acquisition cost rising month on month; about two thirds of orders come from paid social; product is made by a single contract manufacturer; category is crowded with better-funded incumbents.

## Case: "Unbox Robotics" — business type: deeptech
- Compact, AI-assisted robotic package-sorting systems for the warehouses and fulfilment centres of e-commerce and logistics companies. Pune-based, founded 2019. The product is already in live customer sites. Customer problem addressed: sorting cost and accuracy.
- Founders: Pramod Ghadge (CEO) and Shahid Memon (CTO). In the meeting both talked about specific customer sites and what broke during pilots, described failures before being asked, and relied little on the deck. The CEO is still learning to sell to large enterprise procurement teams; hardware hiring in Pune is not yet proven.
- Risks listed by the partners: long enterprise sales cycles; pilot-to-rollout conversion uncertain; hardware margin structure unproven at scale; several better-funded global competitors.
