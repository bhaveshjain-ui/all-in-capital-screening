# FACT SHEETS (set 2). Neutral facts from a past screening. Business type is given for each.

## Case: "HopBox" — business type: dense-network / field operations (quick commerce)
- Hyperlocal delivery of groceries and daily essentials in under ten minutes from small dark stores, operating in one city neighbourhood cluster.
- Founders: Varun Malhotra and Dev Sethi, two former operations managers at a quick-commerce company. They know picking times, rider utilisation and wastage by category. When pressed, neither could describe a path to profitability that did not depend on a large raise to reach density, and they treated that as someone else's problem to solve.
- Risks listed by the partners: capital-intensive model that only works at density; well-funded incumbents with deep pockets; contribution margin negative on the data shown; customer loyalty unproven.

## Case: "Wint Wealth" — business type: other (fintech distribution, retail bonds)
- A platform that makes bond-market products accessible to retail investors in India. Early on it was run almost manually.
- Founder: Ajinkya Kulkarni, founder and CEO. He and his co-founders talked to prospective users directly, explained bonds to them, ran a basic lead-generation website and arranged transactions by hand, even joining intro calls themselves.
- Risks listed by the partners: the early manual model had no obvious scale mechanism; the regulatory perimeter for retail bond distribution was evolving; regulation of retail access to debt products; trust is slow to build where mistakes cost real money; early volumes were small.

## Case: "CampusCredit" — business type: lending / credit
- Buy-now-pay-later and small-ticket credit for college students, underwritten with parent co-signer data and campus ambassador networks.
- Founders: Rohan Iyer, 24, a recent engineering graduate, and a classmate as CTO. Strong on distribution through campus networks. Neither had any experience in credit, collections or risk. Asked about default rates, Iyer dismissed the question as "something the NBFC partner handles".
- Risks listed by the partners: depends on an NBFC partner for the licence and balance sheet; collections from students are hard; unclear credit-bureau data on first-time borrowers; regulatory scrutiny of digital lending.
