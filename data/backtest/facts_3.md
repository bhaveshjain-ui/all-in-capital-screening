# FACT SHEETS (set 3). Neutral facts from a past screening. Business type is given for each.

## Case: "Giva" — business type: consumer brand (D2C jewellery)
- An online-first fine silver jewellery brand founded in 2019, later expanding into gold and lab-grown diamonds and building offline presence.
- Founder: Ishendra Agarwal, co-founder, an IIT Kanpur alumnus. He described repeat purchase, gifting occasions and product margin rather than follower counts. He knew his cost of acquisition by channel, his return rate and the percentage of revenue from repeat customers. (The figures themselves are not given.)
- Risks listed by the partners: inventory and working-capital intensity; heavy reliance on marketplaces and paid channels early; competition from established jewellers going digital; the category could become a discount-led marketplace game.

## Case: "AeroDrop" — business type: deeptech
- Autonomous drone delivery of medical supplies and small parcels over short distances, with a working prototype and a handful of demonstration flights.
- Founders: Dr. Meera Kapoor, an aerospace PhD with a strong research record, and an operations co-founder who joined recently. She spoke in depth on the flight systems. She had less to say about who the paying customer was, who bore liability for a failed delivery, and how regulators would permit routine beyond-visual-line-of-sight operation; she described those as "solved once the tech works".
- Risks listed by the partners: regulatory permission for commercial operations is uncertain on a multi-year horizon; no signed customer; liability and insurance unaddressed.

## Case: "SuperK" — business type: dense-network / field operations (small-town retail)
- A tech-enabled grocery retail network for small towns. It rebrands existing neighbourhood stores under its own name and provides standardised pricing, supply, discounts and digital billing. Founded 2020, operating in small towns of Andhra Pradesh.
- Founders: Anil Thontepu (growth and product work at a Southeast Asian services marketplace) and Neeraj Menta (co-founded a cafeteria-management company; worked in fintech). Both were willing to spend months in small-town stores learning how shopkeepers actually operate. Neither had run a retail network at scale.
- Risks listed by the partners: operationally intense; thin margins; store-owner trust and retention; supply chain in small towns; limited data on same-store sales growth at the time.
